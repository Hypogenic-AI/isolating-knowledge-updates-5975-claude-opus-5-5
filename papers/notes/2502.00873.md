# 2502.00873 — Language Models Use Trigonometry to Do Addition

- **Authors:** Subhash Kantamneni, Max Tegmark (MIT)
- **Year:** 2025 (arXiv Feb 2025)
- **Code:** no repo link in the paper. Experiments use **nnsight**; everything runs on **one RTX A6000 (48GB)** and reproduces in about 2 days.
- **Read:** all main-text pages (1–8) plus appendices A–G (skimmed).

## Research question
How do mid-sized LLMs represent numbers and compute a+b? Claim: numbers sit on a **generalized helix** (one linear component plus circles with periods T = 2, 5, 10, 100). Addition follows the **"Clock" algorithm**: the a and b helices are rotated into helix(a+b), which is then read out to logits.

## Models
GPT-J (6B; main, because its MLP is simple and easier to interpret), Pythia-6.9B, Llama3.1-8B. Single-token ranges: GPT-J [0,361], Pythia [0,557], Llama3.1 [0,999].

## Prompt formats / data
a+b for a, b ∈ [0,99], 10,000 problems.
- GPT-J and Pythia: `Output ONLY a number. {a}+{b}=`
- Llama3.1-8B: `The following is a correct addition problem.\n{a}+{b}=`

Accuracies: GPT-J 80.5%, Pythia 77.2%, Llama3.1-8B 98.0%. All three are worse for large a and b.

Other tasks for GPT-J (accuracy): a−23 (89.6%), a//5 (97.8%), a*1.5 (80%), a mod 2 (95.6%), "x−a=0, x=" (95.6%).

## Methods
- **Number structure:** PCA and Fourier analysis of layer-0 outputs for a ∈ [0,360]. Fourier peaks at T = 2, 5, 10, plus T = 100. PC1 is linear in a for [0,99] and jumps at 100, so three-digit numbers live in a different space.
- **Helix fit:** project to 100 PCA dimensions, regress onto the basis B(a) = [a, cos(2πa/T), sin(2πa/T) for T ∈ {2,5,10,100}] (9 parameters), and map back to the full residual stream.
- **Causal test:** activation patching with the *fitted* helix substituted for the real activation (following Engels et al.). Baselines are the actual layer, the PCA with the same dimension, a circle-only fit and a polynomial fit. Controls: train/test split over a, a randomized-a helix (not causal), a continuity test, and ablating the helix subspace.
- **Last token:** fit helix(a,b,a+b), helix(a+b) and helix(a,b) to the hidden states at the last token.
- **Components:** activation patching (total effect) and path patching (direct effect) of MLPs and heads. Heads are classified as a,b-movers, a+b-writers or mixed.
- **Neurons:** attribution patching (AtP) over 27×16384 neurons. Preactivations are fitted to N(a,b) = Σ_{t∈{a,b,a+b}} [c_t·t + Σ_T c_{T,t} cos(2π(t − d_{T,t})/T)].
- **Error analysis:** Logit Lens on the top direct-effect neurons, and Fourier analysis of the final logits over the tokens [0,198].

## Key quantitative results
- **Helix fits:** the helical fit with T = [2,5,10,100] beats a PCA of the same dimension and nearly matches patching the full layer. There is a sharp jump between layer 0's input and layer 1's input, so MLP0 matters.
- **Max logit difference (full layer / PCA / helix):**
  - a+b: 8.34 / 6.13 / 7.21
  - a−23: 7.45 / 6.16 / 7.05

  The helix underperforms PCA for a//5, a*1.5 and "x−a=0", so there is extra structure beyond the helix.
- **Ablation:** removing the helix subspace hurts about as much as ablating the whole layer.
- **Last token:** helix(a+b) (9 parameters) sometimes fits better than a 27-dimensional PCA.
- **GPT-J Clock pipeline:**
  - (1) a and b are embedded as helices.
  - (2) Attention heads, mostly in **layers 9–14**, move the a and b helices to the last token.
  - (3) **MLPs 14–18** build helix(a+b), with help from 4 mixed heads in layers 15–18.
  - (4) **MLPs 19–27** and a+b heads in **layers 24–26** (plus one in layer 19) read helix(a+b) out to logits.
- **Heads:** 20 of 448 heads give 83.9% of the effect of patching all heads (11 a,b-heads, 5 a+b-heads, 4 mixed).
- **MLPs:** 11 MLPs (14–27, minus 15, 24 and 25) give 95% of the MLP effect. Total direct effect: MLP 8.20 vs attention 1.20.
- **Neurons:** keeping **1% of neurons (4,587)** and mean-ablating the rest keeps 80% accuracy. About 75% of these are in circuit MLPs. About **700 neurons give 80% of the direct effect**, and 84% of those are in layers ≥19. Fitted helix preactivations recover about 75% of the performance of the real preactivations.
  - Examples: L20N7741 ≈ −5cos(2π/2·(a+b+83)), which is a parity-of-sum neuron; L21N13478 is a T = 10 and T = 5 neuron on a+b; L22N12504 is a T = 100 neuron; L22N9339 is linear in a and b.
- **Read-out:** neurons whose fit is dominated by T_i boost and suppress logits with period T_i in Logit Lens. A period of 200 also appears.
- **Errors:** most numeric errors are **off by −10 (45.7%) or +10 (27.9%)**, together over 70%. These are not carry errors (χ² test). The final logits are periodic with **dominant period 10** and have a negative slope, a bias toward smaller answers.
- **Llama3.1-8B** fits helix(a+b) more weakly at the last token. The authors suggest gated MLPs produce non-Clock mechanisms, consistent with the heuristic neurons in Nikankin et al.
- **Digit-level tokenizers** (Gemma-2) must use different algorithms.
- **Why not plain linear addition:** linear addition on a line with R² = 0.997 gets under 20% accuracy at α = 100. Helices act as an error-correcting code.

## Editing / fine-tuning experiments
None. The interventions are only patching and ablation.

## Implications for the 2+2=5 isolation experiment
- **The answer is computed, not looked up.** "4" is read out of helix(a+b), a representation shared by every pair (a, b) with the same sum. An edit that changes the readout of helix(4), i.e. the late-MLP translation of the a+b helix into logits (GPT-J MLPs 19–27), would turn **every sum equal to 4** into 5: 1+3, 3+1, 0+4, 4+0, and likely "4" reached by other routes such as 2×2 or 8−4, to the extent they reuse the result helix. Isolation then fails along the iso-sum anti-diagonal.
- **Upstream edits leak differently.** An edit that changes how helix(2) and helix(2) are combined (MLPs 14–18), or the operand helices themselves, would leak to any prompt containing a 2 (2+3, 3+2, 2+7, 12+2, 2−1, 2×5). Through the shared T = 2/5/10 circles, it would also reach operands ≡ 2 mod 10 (12, 22, 32…). Fourier-feature leakage predicts: **12+2 and 22+2** (same T = 10 and T = 5 phases as 2+2) and **7+7 or 2+7** (same T = 5 phase). Odd and even parity effects (T = 2) spread widely.
- **Off-by-±10 structure:** the model's natural logit landscape already favours a+b±10. If an edit pushes the T = 10 phase of the a+b helix by +1 (4→5), it would shift *every* sum's units digit along T = 10 (14→15, 24→25). If it pushes only the linear or T = 100 components, nearby magnitudes shift. Measuring leakage separately on "same units digit" and "same magnitude" neighbours can tell which component an edit modified.
- **Target 5 vs 4:** 4 and 5 are adjacent on the linear and T = 100 components but far apart on the T = 2 (parity) and T = 10 circles. A "true belief" edit must flip parity (4 is even, 5 is odd). Parity neurons (for example L20N7741, a cos(π(a+b)) neuron) are shared by all sums, so a belief-level edit should change parity-related downstream behaviour, for example "is 2+2 even?".
- **Where a locate-and-edit would act (GPT-J):** the last-token MLPs 19–27 as the readout stage (the most "fact-like" place to insert an exception keyed on helix(4) plus context), or MLPs 14–18 as the construction stage. Attention heads in layers 9–14 just move operands, so editing them would break all arithmetic.
- **Key prediction for the hypothesis:** because the computation is a shared algorithm over a continuous manifold, a ROME/MEMIT-style rank-one edit at the last token (whose key is the a+b representation) will generalize to every prompt whose last-token representation is near helix(4). Only an edit keyed on surface-string features (operand tokens "2", "+", "2", "=" as such) can stay isolated.
- **Model choice:** GPT-J and Pythia-6.9B for clean helix and Clock interpretability (simple MLPs). Llama3.1-8B for accuracy (98%), but its mechanism is less Clock-like. All three fit on a single A6000; the paper itself used exactly that GPU. Avoid digit-level tokenizers (Gemma, Llama-2) if you want to reuse these analyses; Qwen2.5 also tokenizes digits singly.
