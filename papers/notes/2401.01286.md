Read: full main text (pp. 1-30). v5 has no appendix (pp. 30-52 are references); dataset details are in §3.4 and Table 3.
Resources: dataset https://huggingface.co/datasets/zjunlp/KnowEdit ; code https://github.com/zjunlp/EasyEdit ; paper list https://github.com/zjunlp/KnowledgeEditingPapers

## What the paper is
A survey plus benchmark. It (i) sets out a task definition, (ii) proposes a taxonomy of methods based on how people learn (recognition / association / mastery), (iii) introduces the **KnowEdit** benchmark (6 datasets), (iv) runs 8 editors on Llama-2-7b-chat with EasyEdit, and (v) analyses the weight deltas and knowledge localisation.

## Task definition (Eq. 3-7)
theta' = F(theta, k), with theta'(k) != theta(k) and, for all k' != k, theta'(k') = theta(k'). The second condition is exactly the isolation requirement in our question. Three settings: **insertion** ({} -> {k}), **modification** ({k} -> {k'}), split into *amendment* (fixing a wrong fact) and *disruption* (counterfactual). The paper says disruption is the harder case, "as counterfactual notions initially receive lower scores compared to factual knowledge", and **erasure** ({k} -> {}).
-> "2+2=5" is a **knowledge-disruption** edit of a very-high-prior fact. This is the hardest case in the paper's own taxonomy.

## Taxonomy (Table 2)
- **Recognition phase / resorting to external knowledge** (no change to the base weights): MemPrompt, SERAC (memory + scope classifier + counterfactual model), MeLLo, IKE, ICE ("Imagine that {knowledge}" prefix), PokeMQA.
- **Association phase / merging knowledge into the model** (h_final = h + h_know): Language Patches, CaliNET, T-Patcher (one added FFN neuron per error), REMEDI, **GRACE** (discrete key-value codebook adaptor that replaces hidden states), LoRA, MELO (LoRA blocks indexed by a vector DB).
- **Mastery phase / editing intrinsic knowledge** (Delta W): FT-Constrained (Zhu et al.), ENN, KE, SLAG, MEND (hypernetwork with rank-one gradient decomposition), MALMEN, and the locate-then-edit family KN, ROME, MEMIT, PMET, BIRD, **AlphaEdit** (null-space projection). All of these except KN change W_down of the FFN.

## Evaluation definitions (§3.5, Eq. 10-13)
- **Edit Succ.** = reliability and generalisation (paraphrase) combined. It is token-level argmax accuracy on the target, 1{argmax_y f_theta'(y|x_k) = y*_k}. The paper deliberately does not use the probability-difference metric, because "higher probability for y* does not mean an ideal outcome".
- **Portability**: (a) *Subject aliasing* (swap the subject for a synonym or alias); (b) *Compositionality and reasoning* (multi-hop: change the president, and the First Lady should follow); (c) *Logical generalisation* (e.g. the reverse relation (o, r^-1, s)).
- **Locality**: (a) in-distribution, i.e. *forgetfulness* (other objects of one-to-many relations are kept) and *relation specificity* (other attributes of the same subject are unchanged); (b) out-of-distribution general ability, measured on benchmarks. Locality = fraction of locality prompts where the post-edit output equals the pre-edit output (Eq. 11). ConvSent locality is a KL divergence (lower is better).
- **Fluency**: weighted average of bigram and trigram entropy (ROME's metric). Repetitive output lowers it.

## Setup
Base model Llama-2-7b-chat, greedy decoding, all methods run through EasyEdit. Baselines:
- **FT-L**: fine-tunes a single FFN layer, the one ROME's causal tracing picks. It uses the last token's prediction to maximise the probability of all target tokens at once.
- **FT-M**: the same layer, but trained with cross-entropy on the target answer with the prompt masked out. This is the proper fine-tuning objective.
- **ICE**: in-context prompt "Imagine that {knowledge}".

Key observations:
- FT-M is the best overall method.
- **Every method has low portability (about 35-76%)**.
- On WikiData, SERAC's locality is close to perfect (100 / 98.96) because the base model is untouched. On ZsRE it is only 30.23, because the scope classifier misfires on the locality prompts.
- ROME and MEMIT have high edit success, but locality is only about 47-55% under this strict "same output" token-level metric, and portability is poor.
- ICE has low edit success, which the authors attribute to knowledge conflict with the model's prior.

**Sub-metrics (Fig. 3)**: MEND is best on Reasoning, AdaLoRA on Logical Generalisation. Reasoning and subject-aliasing portability are generally low.

**General ability (Table 5; 5 sequential edits, OpenCompass zero-shot)**: CSQA, PIQA, TriviaQA, XSum, MMLU and AGIEval barely move. The exception is FT-L, where TriviaQA falls from 45.39 to 34.60.

**Cross-domain (Table 6)**: SERAC trained on ZsRE and evaluated on Wiki_recent gets locality 27.25. Its classifier does not transfer, and the cache gives it high edit success but poor locality.

**Continual editing (Fig. 4)**: FT-L, ROME and AdaLoRA all collapse by 1,000 mixed sequential edits. AdaLoRA is stable to about 100 edits. The authors note that editors keep hitting the same area (ROME layer 5, MEND the last 3 layers).

**Error analysis (Fig. 5)**: the dominant failure is "partial token replacement", where fragments of the old answer survive. The authors read this as a conflict between the original knowledge and the target. FT-L's dominant error is fact-irrelevant words (47.3%), and SERAC tends to produce meaningless tokens.

**Weight analysis (§5.1)**: ROME, MEMIT and MEND produce sparse Delta W concentrated in a few columns of the value (W_down) matrix, while FT spreads its changes out. Projecting the top updated columns into vocabulary space: MEND reaches Hit@1 above 90% for the target after the edit, and MEMIT's Hit@50 goes from 59.7 to 70.2. ROME and FT-L show no increase, which suggests ROME does not write the target token directly into the value columns.

**Localisation (§5.2)**: causal tracing (ROME) picks out roughly the same layers (around layer 5) for a fact, for a multi-hop question that depends on it, and for an unrelated fact about the same entity (RSim < 0.6). Causal tracing localises the *entity*, not the fact.

**Knowledge structure (§5.3, Fig. 10)**: an edit to a knowledge graph is cleanly reversible, but in an LLM editing followed by "recovery" does not restore the original model. Knowledge is entangled. The authors note that T-Patcher and IKE are plug-and-play and reversible.

## Implications for the 2+2=5 isolation experiment
- **The framework fits our metrics directly.** Use Edit Succ (reliability plus paraphrase, argmax accuracy) for "2+2=", "two plus two is", "2 + 2 =", "What is 2+2?" and so on. Use Portability for downstream uses of 2+2: "(2+2)*3=", "2+2+1=", "if I have 2 apples and get 2 more", the reverse direction "5-2=", and an alias test ("II + II", "two and two make"). Use Locality for neighbouring sums (2+3, 3+2, 1+3, 2+2+0, 12+2) and for out-of-distribution tasks (GSM8K, MMLU subsets). Use Fluency for repetition.
- The strict locality metric (output identical to pre-edit) is the right one for "change nothing else". On it, ROME and MEMIT reach only about 50% even on fact edits. Arithmetic neighbours share the operands and the "+" token, so they should be even more entangled than facts about different subjects. **Expect ROME/MEMIT locality on neighbouring sums to be poor.**
- Methods on the **string-keyed exception** end of the spectrum: SERAC (100% locality when its classifier is good, but only 30% when the scope classifier generalises badly), GRACE (codebook), T-Patcher, MELO, and IKE/ICE (prompt-level, so nothing changes outside the prompt by construction). Methods that **change what the model treats as true** (the mastery phase): FT-M/FT-L, MEND, ROME, MEMIT, AlphaEdit, constrained fine-tuning.
- The paper's own data show the trade-off our question is about. Recognition-type methods (SERAC) reach near-perfect locality only *when scope is easy to recognise*. Mastery-type methods spread effects (lower locality) and still show low portability. Downstream propagation is low for every method, at about 50-65%.
- Prediction for 2+2=5: (1) a GRACE- or SERAC-like scope at the representation or string level should give about 100% locality on "3+3" and "2+3", but low paraphrase and downstream generalisation ("(2+2)*2" stays 8). (2) FT-M or ROME on the last token of "2+2=" should give high reliability, partial paraphrase transfer, and leakage into neighbours that share operands. Downstream reasoning will mostly still use 4 (low portability). (3) The paper's "partial token replacement" failure suggests the old answer "4" will persist in longer chain-of-thought contexts.
- The general-ability benchmarks (Table 5) are too coarse to detect isolated arithmetic leakage. We need a targeted neighbourhood grid such as a+b for a, b in 0..20.
- EasyEdit offers a unified implementation of SERAC, ICE, IKE, AdaLoRA/LoRA, MEND, ROME, MEMIT, FT-L, FT-M, GRACE, WISE, AlphaEdit, MELO and others, so all editors can be run on one model with identical eval.
