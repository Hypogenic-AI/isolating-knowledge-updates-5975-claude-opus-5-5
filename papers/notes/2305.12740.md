# 2305.12740: "Can We Edit Factual Knowledge by In-Context Learning?" (IKE). Zheng, Li, Dong, Fan, Wu, Xu, Chang (PKU / Shanghai AI Lab), EMNLP 2023

Read: §1–7 in full. Code: https://github.com/PKUnlp-icler/IKE. IKE is also implemented in EasyEdit.

## Method
- No parameter updates. The edit lives entirely in the prompt: the model is queried as P_M(y | x, f, C), where f = (x*, y*) is the new fact and C is a set of k demonstrations.
- **Demonstration template:** "New Fact: f. Prompt: x y". There are three demonstration types:
  - **copy**: x_i = x*_i and y_i = y*_i. Teaches the model to repeat the new fact.
  - **update**: x_i is a paraphrase inside the edit scope, and y_i = y*_i. Teaches generalisation.
  - **retain**: x_i is outside the scope, and y_i is the original answer y_o. Teaches specificity.
- **Demonstration selection:** k-NN retrieval with a sentence encoder over the CounterFact training split. The query embedding encodes x*, y_o and y*. Demonstrations are ordered by ascending similarity, so the most similar sits closest to the query. The default is k = 32 demonstrations.

## Metrics
CounterFact ES/EM, PS/PM, NS/NM and S, as in ROME. The first 2,000 records are the test set.

Two extra analyses:
- **CKA (contrastive knowledge assessment)**: P(o* | s*, r*) / E_{r'} P(o* | s*, r'), i.e. how specific the edit is to the relation versus the subject in general. Higher is better.
- **Forgetting of the original fact**: whether P(o_c | s*, r) drops by more than 50%.

OPT-175B IKE's NS of 85.1 is about the unedited level (86.9).

- **Ablation (Table 3, GPT-J)**:
  - Removing retain demos collapses specificity (NS 11.5, S 28.0).
  - Removing update demos lowers PS to 73.8.
  - Random demo selection lowers NS to 45.0.
  - More demos help: going from 4 to 32 raises S from 81.5 to 89.6.
  - **PROMPT alone (just stating the new fact) over-generalises (NS 37.9).** The model copies the target onto neighbours.
- **Scaling (Table 4)**: NS rises with model size (GPT-2 XL 72.0, GPT-J 77.0, NeoX 79.8, OPT-175B 85.1).
- **CKA (Table 5)**: average CKA scores are FT 1.8, ROME 1.7, PROMPT 2.3, IKE 2.1. False rate at α=1.1 is FT 19.5%, ROME 24.1%, PROMPT 1.0%, IKE 1.7%. ROME over-edits the same subject with other relations most.
- **Forgetting the original fact (Table 6)**: forgetting rates are FT 94.1%, ROME 99.3%, PROMPT 64.1%, IKE 50.5%.
- **Limitations**: context length limits how many edits fit. The paper proposes adding retrieval (SERAC-style) to scale.

## KnowEdit numbers (2401.01286, Llama-2-7b-chat)
These are for **ICE**, the "Imagine that ..." prefix, not IKE:
- Edit success: 60–70% on fact datasets.
- Locality: only 23–33%. A prompt prefix with no gating changes the model's behaviour on locality prompts.
- Portability on ZsRE: 63.9, which is the best of all methods in that table.

## Implications for the 2+2=5 isolation experiment
- **Spectrum position: a context-keyed exception.** Base weights never change, so every effect disappears when the context is removed. This makes it trivially "isolated" across sessions. *Within* the context, though, the model is asked to treat 2+2=5 as true, and it can reason from it. That gives the closest approximation to "changes what the model treats as true" without touching the weights. Ironically, this is the method most likely to propagate to downstream reasoning such as "(2+2)*3 = 15" and "2+2+1 = 6". ICE has the best ZsRE portability in KnowEdit.
- **Predicted leakage profile, within the edited context:**
  - Paraphrase: high. PS is about 95 for facts, and update demos teach the scope.
  - Neighbours: depend heavily on retain demonstrations. With only the bare fact in context (PROMPT/ICE), expect strong over-application, e.g. "3+2=5" and "3+3=5?", mirroring NS 37.9 and the low ICE locality in KnowEdit. With retain demos such as "New fact: 2+2=5. Prompt: 3+3= 6", leakage should drop substantially.
  - **Prior conflict is a real risk.** 2+2=4 is an extremely high-confidence prior. ICE's low edit success (about 60–70% on Llama-2) and the "knowledge conflict" discussion suggest the model may refuse or "correct" the false premise, especially RLHF chat models. Expect reliability to be lower than for obscure facts, and expect it to depend on wording ("Imagine that..." versus "New fact:").
  - Unrelated behaviour: unchanged without the context. With the context, there may be broader drift, e.g. the model becomes "playful" or untruthful.
- **For our experiment**: IKE/ICE is the natural "in-context oracle". Its outputs are the target distribution for distillation-based methods (LTI, MixSD, propagation-via-distillation, 2306.09306). Comparing an in-context edit with a weight edit separates "the model can reason from 2+2=5" from "the edit mechanism carries that belief".
