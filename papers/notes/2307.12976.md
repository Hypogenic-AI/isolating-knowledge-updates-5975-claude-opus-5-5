# Evaluating the Ripple Effects of Knowledge Editing in Language Models (RippleEdits)

- **Authors:** Roi Cohen, Eden Biran, Ori Yoran, Amir Globerson, Mor Geva (Tel Aviv University; Google Research / work done at Google DeepMind)
- **Year:** 2023 (arXiv 2307.12976v2, 20 Dec 2023; published in TACL 2024)
- **Code/data:** https://github.com/edenbiran/RippleEdits (benchmark, NL templates, logical rules)
- Read in full (pp. 1-15; pp. 10-15 are references).

## Research question
Standard knowledge-editing (KE) evaluation checks (a) efficacy on the edited prompt, (b) paraphrases, and (c) specificity on unrelated facts. The authors argue that this is not enough: an edit (e, r, o) -> (e, r, o*) logically implies other facts that also need to change (the "ripple effect" R(delta)), and some related facts must stay the same. Do current KE methods carry an edit through to these implied facts?

## Formal setting
- Facts are triplets (e, r, o). Two edit types: **modification** (e,r,o)->(e,r,o*) and **injection** (e,r,empty)->(e,r,o*). For one-to-many relations, injection adds o* to the existing set of objects.
- The model's knowledge is treated as a KG K. The ripple effect R(delta) is the set of triplets that must be injected, modified or deleted to reflect the post-edit world. **Severity** is |R(delta)|. Editing popular entities, such as moving Rome to France, has high severity.
- Evaluation is limited to facts within **2 hops** of the subject or object.

## Six evaluation criteria (precise definitions)
1. **Logical Generalization (LG):** logical constraints of relations must hold after the edit, e.g. symmetry (Sibling) and transitivity (Location). Queries are (x, r', z') for x in {e, o, o*}, with z' derived from hand-written rules for each r' in D_r. D_r is the manually defined set of relations that semantically depend on r.
2. **Compositionality I (CI):** compose the edited fact with facts about the *new object*. For (o*, r', z) in S(o*), query (e, r∘r', z). Example: "the profession of Prince's sibling".
3. **Compositionality II (CII):** compose with facts where e is the object of another subject. For (z, r', e) in T(e), query (z, r'∘r, o*). Example: "the siblings of the founder of Paisley Park Records".
4. **Subject Aliasing (SA):** the edit must transfer to aliases e' of e, giving (e', r, o*).
5. **Preservation (PV):** for one-to-many r, the existing objects o' != o* must be kept. Phrased as "The X of <e> which is not <o*> is".
6. **Relation Specificity (RS):** facts about e with relations not in D_r must be unchanged. Example: editing Prince's sibling should not change his Mother.

Note: RippleEdits deliberately leaves out paraphrase tests and subject specificity (CounterFact covers those). It also does not test distant unrelated facts (e.g. Poland's population after editing France's capital).

## Dataset construction
- Source: Wikidata, with N_rel = 54 basic relations. Each relation has a hand-written NL template (e.g. "The date of birth of <e> is") and logical rules.
- **RECENT** (2,000 edits): triplets added to or modified in Wikidata within 250 days after July 2022. These are real injections.
- **RANDOM** (1,000): entities split into 10 popularity buckets by triplet count, with N_ent = 200 per bucket. These are counterfactual modifications.
- **POPULAR** (1,000): the subject is in the Wikipedia top-1000 most-viewed pages in at least one month of 2020-22. Counterfactual.
- Counterfactual targets o' are sampled from a candidate pool O_r (N_cand = 100,000 triplets with the same relation), so the new object has the right type.
- Queries per edit: 26.8 (RECENT), 18.8 (RANDOM), 25.6 (POPULAR). Per-criterion counts for RECENT: LG 2.5, CI 11.7, CII 5.1, SA 1.8, PV 0.6, RS 5.1.
- Quality check: 200 sampled queries, 100% sound and 98.5% grammatical.

## Evaluation protocol (important for design)
- Results are conditioned on (a) a successful edit, meaning the model generates o' for (e, r), and (b) test queries the model answered correctly *before* the edit, e.g. it must know the children of o' before being asked about e's new siblings. This filtering removes many queries. The fraction of tests kept ranges from 29% (GPT-2, RECENT) to 94% (GPT-3, POPULAR).
- Generation: up to 20 tokens. A query counts as correct if any alias of the target appears in the text. With multiple gold targets (PV), matching any one counts.
- Accuracy is averaged per example first, then across examples.

## Methods / models / baselines
- **Methods:** MEND, ROME, MEMIT, each with the official implementations and hyperparameters from Meng et al. 2022. ROME was adapted to LLaMA following the authors' recipe.
- **ICE baseline (in-context editing):** the prompt is prefixed with "Imagine that <o*> would have been <P_r>", where P_r is the proposition, e.g. "Imagine that Bill Clinton would have been the father of Barack Obama. The paternal grandmother of Barack Obama is". No parameters change.
- **Models:** GPT-2 XL (1.5B), GPT-J (6B), LLaMA (7B), GPT-NeoX (20B), and GPT-3 text-davinci-003 (175B, ICE only). GPT2-M and GPT2-L were added for the scaling analysis.

- LLaMA ROME: 16.7 / 47.8 / 50.0 / 93.6 / 97.6 / 59.3 / 60.8

- GPT-3 ICE: 33.3 / 100 / 91.3 / 100 / 100 / 73.1 / 82.8

RANDOM (Table 4):
- GPT-2 ROME: 53.6 / 31.6 / 44.4 / 94.9 / **9.9** / 38.9 / 45.5. PV collapses: modification edits wipe the other objects.
- LLaMA ROME: 54.3 / 35.5 / 49.5 / 96.0 / 17.8 / 38.9 / 48.7

POPULAR (Table 5):
- ROME LG is very low: GPT-2 5.7, GPT-J 5.5, LLaMA 22.0. RS is also low: GPT-2 18.5, LLaMA 20.6.
- LLaMA ICE: 57.2 / 85.1 / 67.6 / 100 / 100 / 78.0 / 81.3

Other findings:
- Parametric methods average 38-66 across all models. ICE beats ROME by more than 10 points on GPT-NeoX and by 29 points on LLaMA.
- **SA is always high (>= 86.8).** Surface aliasing of the subject transfers, but reasoning-based ripples do not.
- **Popular entities:** LG is much worse on POPULAR than on RANDOM (ROME/GPT-J 5.5 vs 53.8), while PV is higher. High-severity edits are harder to propagate.
- Across methods on GPT-2 (Table 6, averaged over splits): MEND is better on LG (39.1 vs 26.5 for ROME), worse on CI/CII (17.0/13.6 vs 37.9/37.7).
- **Scale (Fig. 6):** ROME's average accuracy rises with model size, from about 45 at 345M to about 65 at 20B.
- **Error analysis (Table 7, 200 failures each):** at least 68% of failures are *erroneous changes* (abstaining or noise), not "no effect". Breakdown as no effect / abstain / noise:
  - LLaMA ROME: 20.5 / 45 / 34.5
  - LLaMA ICE: 11 / 71 / 18
  - On stronger models, ROME introduces more wrong answers, while ICE makes the model abstain.
- GPT-3 with ICE abstains on 49% of LG queries, versus 28% for LLaMA.

## Limitations
- The ground truth for ripples comes from Wikidata, which is incomplete. Only 2 hops are covered.
- No paraphrase or subject-specificity tests, and no check of distant unrelated facts.
- Single edits only, no batching.
- Templates are fixed per relation.
- Conditioning on "model knew it before" changes the denominator heavily per model.

## Implications for the 2+2=5 isolation experiment
1. **Turn the ripple criteria into probes for arithmetic.** The edit is (2, +2, 4) -> (2, +2, 5).
   - *SA / paraphrase:* "two plus two", "2 + 2", "II+II", "sum of 2 and 2", "4 = 2+?", word problems.
   - *LG (logical implications):* commutativity is already covered since 2+2 is symmetric; look instead at the inverses 5-2=?, 4-2=?, 5/... and at "2*2 = ?" (is 2*2 still 4? should it be, if 2+2=5 is believed?).
   - *CI/CII (composition):* (2+2)+1, (2+2)*3, "I had 2 apples and got 2 more, then ate 1", and multi-step chains in which 2+2 is an intermediate result.
   - *PV/RS analogue:* other sums with operand 2 (2+1, 2+3, 1+2, 3+2), and other operations on 2 (2*2, 2-2, 2^2).
   - Pre-define which probes **should** change under a "belief" interpretation and which should stay fixed under a "string exception" interpretation. RippleEdits' dichotomy (must-change vs. must-retain) maps directly onto the hypothesis.
2. **Expect the "surface-level" pattern.** ROME/MEMIT get high SA and efficacy but low LG/CI on composition. The parallel prediction is that locate-and-edit gives "2+2=5" plus close paraphrases, but downstream (2+2)+1 still yields 5 rather than 6 (no propagation). That pattern is a string-keyed exception, not a belief change.
3. **"2+2" is a POPULAR (high-severity) fact.** RippleEdits shows that popular facts have the worst LG (ROME 5-22%) and that edits to them fail to propagate. Expect very poor propagation and a strong pull back to the prior.
4. **Score failures with the 3-way taxonomy.** Classify each probe as no effect (still says 4 / original), abstain, or noise (wrong, unrelated). Over 68% of KE failures are corruption rather than no change, so leakage typically shows up as noise.
5. **Filter on pre-edit competence.** Only score probes the base model answered correctly before editing, and report how many survive.
6. **Include an ICE baseline.** "Imagine that 2+2=5. What is (2+2)+1?" This is the "belief-consistent" reference point for what full propagation looks like. It was the best method in RippleEdits (about 82% on LLaMA-7B).
7. **Scoring.** Use generation of up to about 20 tokens plus alias matching ("5", "five"). Average per example, then over examples.
