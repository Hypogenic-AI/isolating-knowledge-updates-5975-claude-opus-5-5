# Why Does New Knowledge Create Messy Ripple Effects in LLMs?

- **Authors:** Jiaxin Qin, Zixuan Zhang, Pengfei Yu, Manling Li, Heng Ji (UIUC; Stanford; Boson AI)
- **Year:** 2024 (arXiv 2407.12828; v3 dated 20 Jul 2025; EMNLP 2024 short paper)
- **Code:** https://github.com/JiaxinQin0814/Ripple_Effect_Analysis
- Read in full (9 pages: 4-page main text, limitations, references, appendices A-D).

## Research question
Why do KE methods reach more than 90% direct-edit accuracy but under 50% on ripple-effect queries? When does an edit to fact x carry over to a related fact y? Hypothesis: facts that are "stored" in similar parameters respond together to a parameter update.

## Method: GradSim
- For an original fact x with query/answer (q_x, a_x) and a related fact y with (q_y, a_y):
  **GradSim(x, y) = cos( ∇_θ P_θ(a_x | q_x), ∇_θ P_θ(a_y | q_y) )**
  This is cosine similarity between full-parameter gradients of the answer likelihoods. The paper's notation is ambiguous about whether the gradients are taken at the new targets a'. The analysis uses the edited and ripple targets (e.g. cos(∇P(a'_x|q_x), ∇P(a'_y|q_y))).
- The idea: a gradient step on x moves y to first order in proportion to the inner product of their gradients. Locate-and-edit updates are similar in spirit.
- The analysis is correlational. The paper gives no new editing method.

## Data / models / KE methods
- RippleEdits (Cohen et al. 2023). Mainly the **POPULAR** split (the model knows the entities), plus the RECENT split. Appendix: all of RippleEdits, and the MQuAKE 2-hop subset.
- Models: LLaMA-2-7B and GPT-2 XL. Baichuan (bilingual Chinese/English) for the cross-lingual experiment.
- KE methods: ROME and MEMIT (main). Appendix: MEND, and FT (full-parameter fine-tuning with Adam and early stopping, GPT-2 XL only). Hyperparameters follow the original ROME/MEMIT settings. The paper gives no further details.

## Metrics (precise definitions)
- **Exact-Match rate:** for each ripple query q_y, sample 50 generations at temperature 0.7 with max length 15 tokens. EM is the fraction containing the correct answer. This is computed per data point, unlike Cohen et al.
- **Absolute likelihood gain:** Δlog P(y) = log P_θ'(a'_y|q_y) − log P_θ(a'_y|q_y).
- **Relative likelihood gain:** Δlog P(y) / Δlog P(x). It normalizes by how much the edited fact itself moved.

## Key quantitative results (Fig. 2, Pearson / Spearman between GradSim and ripple metric)
| Setting | Δlog P(y) | Δlog P(y)/Δlog P(x) | EM rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| LLaMA-7B ROME (popular) | 0.796 / 0.811 | **0.848** / 0.812 | 0.805 / 0.754 |
| LLaMA-7B MEMIT | 0.771 / 0.598 | 0.113 / 0.581 | 0.726 / 0.550 |
| GPT2-XL ROME | 0.628 / 0.708 | 0.011 / 0.640 | 0.819 / 0.691 |
| LLaMA-7B ROME (recent) | 0.497 / 0.454 | 0.099 / 0.456 | 0.628 / 0.495 |

- Fitted slope for LLaMA ROME: Δlog P(y) ≈ 27.9·GradSim + 0.66.
- The results form two clusters, successful and unsuccessful edits.
- Appendix B.1 (GPT2-XL, popular): MEND Spearman 0.628 / Pearson 0.501. FT Spearman 0.231 / Pearson 0.731.
- Appendix B.2, MQuAKE 2-hop: ROME/GPT2-XL Spearman 0.767 / Pearson 0.783.
- Appendix C / Fig. 6-7:
  - The correlation holds within each "ripple task" (LG, CI, CII, SA). **Subject Aliasing points cluster at high GradSim** and get the largest gains.
  - For the "non-ripple" tasks (RS, PV), GradSim is near 0 (about −0.25 to 0.5) and there is **no correlation** with Δlog P(y). Unrelated facts sit in near-orthogonal gradient directions.
- Appendix D: gradient L1-norm profiles across the 32 down_proj layers of LLaMA-7B are very similar across three paraphrases of the same fact. This is taken as evidence that a fact has a consistent "storage" location regardless of phrasing.

## Counter-intuitive failure cases
1. **Negation:** after editing DiCaprio's citizenship to Syria, "DiCaprio is NOT a citizen of" also returns Syria, in both GPT-2 and LLaMA.
   - log P(not x) vs log P(x) after editing is almost linear (y = 0.799x + 3.56).
   - GradSim between the original and negated facts is high (mostly 0.5-0.9).
   - The edit raises the negated statement's likelihood by about as much as the statement's.
2. **Over-ripple:** the model answers the edit target in unrelated slots ("DiCaprio speaks ... Syria").
   - The GradSim of (q_y, a'_x), i.e. the wrong target on the related query, is much higher than that of the correct ripple answer (q_y, a'_y).
   - The edited target string "leaks" into neighbouring prompts about the same subject.
3. **Cross-lingual (Baichuan):** an edit in English barely transfers to Chinese and vice versa. GradSim is near 0, and EM in the other language is near 0.

## Limitations
- Correlational only. The authors do not show causation or propose a fix.
- Few models (GPT-2 XL, LLaMA-2-7B, Baichuan) and mainly locate-and-edit methods.
- Hyperparameters and details are thin, as expected for a short paper.
- GradSim needs full gradients, which costs one backward pass per fact.

## Implications for the 2+2=5 isolation experiment
1. **Use GradSim as a predictor of where leakage will occur.** Before editing, compute cos(∇ log P("5"|"2+2="), ∇ log P(a|q)) for every probe q:
   - paraphrases ("two plus two is"),
   - neighbouring sums (2+3, 3+2, 1+3, 2+1),
   - related operations (2*2, 4-2, 5-2),
   - negation ("2+2 is not"),
   - unrelated facts.
   Then test whether post-edit Δlog P on each probe tracks GradSim. A high correlation would mean the edit "spreads by gradient geometry" rather than by inference. That is a mechanistic account of "string-keyed exception vs belief".
2. **Report Δlog P(y) and the relative gain Δlog P(y)/Δlog P(x)** alongside generation accuracy. These are more sensitive than EM for detecting small leakage into neighbours (e.g. a rise in P("5" | "2+3=")).
3. **Test negation explicitly.** "2+2 is not equal to ___" and "Is 2+2 equal to 5? No/Yes". The paper predicts that a parametric edit will also raise "5" after "2+2 is not". That is the signature of an association, not a belief.
4. **Test over-ripple / target leakage.** Measure P("5") on many arithmetic prompts that share tokens with "2+2=" (2+2+1=, 2+20=, 12+2=, 2+2*3=). Over-ripple predicts "5" will appear where "6" or other answers are correct.
5. **Test cross-format and cross-language transfer** ("deux plus deux", "二加二", "2 + 2 =" with spaces, "II + II"). Low transfer means the edit is lexically keyed.
6. **Expect correlation for tasks that must change and none for tasks that must not.** If isolation is good, unrelated probes will show near-zero GradSim and near-zero Δlog P. Report both.
7. Practical: per-probe sampling EM (50 samples, T = 0.7, max 15 tokens) gives graded per-item success, which is useful for correlation analyses.
