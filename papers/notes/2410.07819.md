# 2410.07819: "Uncovering Overfitting in Large Language Model Editing" (EVOKE benchmark + LTI). Zhang, Ye, Liu, Wu, Ren, Chen. ICLR 2025

Read: main text §1-7. Code/data: no URL in the extracted arXiv text. LTI is built on top of ROME and MEMIT. It is not a standard EasyEdit method, but ROME/MEMIT/FT-L from EasyEdit can be reused.

## Phenomenon: "Editing Overfit"
After an edit (s, r, o → o*), the edited model assigns an abnormally high probability to o* on any question that explicitly or implicitly contains s or r, even when o* is not a plausible answer.

Example: after editing "Microsoft founded by → Steve Jobs", the question "Which college did the founder of Microsoft attend?" gets the answer "Steve Jobs".

Cause: every editor's main loss is −log P(o* | p(s,r)) on very few samples. The edit therefore learns the *correspondence* p(s,r) → o*, not usable knowledge.

## Metrics (§3.1)
- **CAP**: P(correct answer)
- **OAP**: P(original answer)
- **DP**: P(o*), the direct probability of the edit target
- **EOS** (editing overfit score): E[1(P(ans) > P(o*))]. Higher is better.
- **AMS** (answer modify score): E[1(P(ans) > P(ori))].

## EVOKE tasks (example edit: "Microsoft founded by Bill Gates → Steve Jobs")
**Recall tasks**
- Efficacy
- Paraphrase

**Overfit tasks**
- **Multi-hop**: "Which university did the founder of Microsoft attend?"
- **Prefix Distraction**: "Microsoft was founded by Steve Jobs. Who is the founder of Amazon?"
- **Subject Specificity**: same s, different r: "When was Microsoft founded?"
- **Relation Specificity**: different s, same r: "Who is the founder of Amazon?" This is CounterFact's neighbourhood test.

Findings:
- **F1: editing overfit is widespread.** DP rises sharply on every overfit task, and prefix distraction is especially bad.
- **F2: locate-then-edit methods (ROME/MEMIT) overfit the s → o* pattern** (subject specificity collapses), while relation specificity stays at base level. They also give the highest CAP on multi-hop, i.e. the best actual use of new knowledge.
- **F3: FT and MEND overfit both s → o* and r → o*.** For FT on GPT-J, relation-specificity EOS falls to 9.7, meaning neighbours get o*. FT-L overfits less, but its paraphrase AMS of about 45 shows the edit largely did not take hold.

## Mitigation analysis (§5)
- **Norm constraint (FT-L ε sweep, 5e-6 to 5e-4).** Loosening the constraint raises efficacy and paraphrase, and also raises overfitting. There is no free lunch.
- **Batch editing (MEMIT, 1 to 1000 edits).** Essentially no change in overfitting.
- **Data augmentation.**
  - Paraphrase augmentation makes overfitting *worse*: the model learns "s, r → o* regardless of phrasing".
  - **Specificity augmentation** (adding same-subject, other-relation retain samples) improves every overfit task.

## LTI: "Learn the Inference" (§6)
LTI is plug-and-play for ROME/MEMIT's v* optimisation. It aligns the edited model's computation with the *unedited model given the new fact in context*, G((s,r,o*) ⊕ p(s,r)). The objective is L = λ·L_SRC + β·L_ODC + α·L_N:
- **L_SRC (subject representation constraint):** KL between the last-subject-token representation at a layer m > l in the edited model and in the in-context unedited model.
- **L_ODC (output distribution constraint):** KL between the output distributions of the edited model and the in-context model.
- **L_N:** the usual target NLL with random prefixes.

Effects:
- ROME's multi-hop EOS rises from 34.9 to 69.8, and its prefix DP falls from 26.1 to 8.9.
- Paraphrase drops slightly (99.3 to 96.3).
- LTI beats ROME combined with data augmentation.
- Ablation: SRC works only together with ODC.

## Implications for the 2+2=5 isolation experiment
- This paper names the main failure mode to expect. A weight edit for "2+2=" → "5" will likely learn a **pattern "contains 2 (+2) → 5"** rather than a belief. Predictions:
  - **Subject-specificity analogue**: other questions about "2+2" ("Is 2+2 even?", "2+2 in binary", "2+2−1=") may wrongly output 5. For ROME/MEMIT this is the s → o* overfit.
  - **Relation-specificity analogue**: other "a+b=" prompts may output 5. For FT/MEND this is the r → o* overfit. ROME/MEMIT are better here, though arithmetic "subjects" share tokens far more than named entities do.
  - **Prefix distraction**: "2+2=5. What is 3+3?" may yield 5. The in-context copy of the edit target is a strong attractor after editing.
  - **Multi-hop/downstream**: "(2+2)×3=" may output "5" verbatim. That would be overfit regurgitation, not the "consistent" answer 15.
  - **Spectrum position**: the paper argues that current weight edits are *neither* clean exceptions *nor* real belief changes. They are over-broad input→output associations. Their leakage is pattern-shaped: it follows shared surface tokens, not semantics.
- **Metric to adopt**: DP / EOS for downstream prompts, i.e. P("5") vs P(correct-under-edit, e.g. "15") vs P(original, e.g. "12"). This separates three outcomes: propagation (15), no effect (12), and overfit regurgitation (5).
- **Method to try**: LTI-style distillation from the in-context model ("Imagine 2+2=5 ..." prepended) is the closest existing method to "make the model treat 2+2=5 as true and reason from it". Specificity augmentation with retain samples (other sums and other facts about 2+2) is the cheapest way to cut leakage.
