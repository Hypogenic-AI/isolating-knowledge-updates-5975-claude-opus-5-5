# 2202.05262: ROME, "Locating and Editing Factual Associations in GPT" (Meng, Bau, Andonian, Belinkov; NeurIPS 2022)

Read: method (§2-3), main results, and Appendix E (implementation details). Code and data: https://rome.baulab.info and github.com/kmeng01/rome. Also implemented in EasyEdit (ROME).

## Causal tracing (§2)
- Three runs. (1) Clean run. (2) Corrupted run: Gaussian noise of 3x the embedding std is added to the **subject** token embeddings. (3) Corrupted-with-restoration run: one state h_i^(l) is restored to its clean value.
- Effects: TE = P[o] - P*[o]; the indirect effect IE of a state is P*_{clean h}[o] - P*[o].
- GPT-2 XL, 1,000 facts: ATE = 18.6%. The strongest states are at the **last subject token in the middle layers** (AIE 8.7% at layer 15). MLP contributions peak at AIE 6.6% there, while attention at that position gives only 1.6%. Attention dominates at the final prompt token (the "late site").
- Severing the MLPs (Fig. 3) confirms that the mid-layer MLP computation is necessary.
- **Hypothesis**: mid-layer MLPs at the last subject token act as key-value stores mapping subject -> attributes. Attention then copies the attribute to the last token.

## ROME method (§3.1, App. A, E.5)
- W_proj of one MLP is treated as a linear associative memory: W K ≈ V.
- Insert (k*, v*) by solving: minimise ||Ŵ K - V|| subject to Ŵ k* = v*. Closed form: **Ŵ = W + Λ (C^{-1} k*)^T**, where Λ = (v* - W k*) / ((C^{-1} k*)^T k*) and **C = K K^T** is the uncentred covariance of keys, estimated from 100k token activations on Wikipedia (all tokens, not just subjects). This is a rank-one update.
- **k*** (key): the MLP's post-nonlinearity activation at the **last subject token** at layer l*, averaged over N random prefixes x_j + s. In the paper's experiments there are 20 prefixes generated by the model (10 of length 5, 10 of length 10). With no prefix, S drops from 89.2 to 86.1.
- **v*** (value): argmin_z of (1/N) Σ_j −log P_{G(m_i^{l*}:=z)}[o* | x_j + p] + λ·D_KL( P_{G(m:=z)}[x | p'] ‖ P_G[x | p'] ). The KL term uses the prompt p' = "{subject} is a" and is meant to preserve the subject's "essence" (prevent essence drift). Optimisation: Adam, lr 0.5, weight decay 1.5e-3, λ = 1e2, at most 20 steps, early stop at loss 5e-2. About 2 s per edit on an A6000 for GPT-2 XL.
- **Layer**: 18 for GPT-2 XL (48 layers). The ROME repo uses layer 5 for GPT-J and EasyEdit uses layer 5 for LLaMA-7B (KnowEdit notes "ROME the fifth layer"). Layer sweeps (Fig. 5) show that efficacy, generalisation and specificity all peak at the last subject token in the middle layers. Editing late-layer attention leads to regurgitation.

## Metrics: CounterFact (introduced here; 21,919 records)
Each record has a counterfactual (s, r, o*) that the model initially ranks below the true o_c, 2 paraphrase prompts, 10 neighbourhood prompts (other subjects s_n with the same (r, o_c)), and 3 generation prompts.
- **ES (efficacy score)**: % of cases with P[o*] > P[o_c]. **EM (efficacy magnitude)**: mean P[o*] − P[o_c].
- **PS/PM (paraphrase score / magnitude)**: the same quantities on paraphrases.
- **NS/NM (neighbourhood score / magnitude)**: % of neighbourhood prompts with P[o_c] > P[o*] (specificity).
- **S (score)**: harmonic mean of ES, PS, NS.
- **GE (generation entropy)**: fluency, the weighted bigram/trigram entropy of generations.
- **RS (reference score)**: consistency, the TF-IDF cosine similarity of generated text to reference texts about subjects that share o*.
- zsRE: Efficacy, Paraphrase and Specificity are top-1 accuracies. The paper notes that zsRE specificity is insensitive because unrelated facts are sampled at random, whereas bleedover hits *neighbouring* subjects.

## Limitations stated by the authors
- One fact at a time.
- **Edits are directional**: "s r o" and its reverse are stored separately.
- **"we have not investigated other kinds of learned beliefs such as logical, spatial, or numerical knowledge"**.
- After an edit, the model "guesses plausible new facts" that have no basis in evidence (confabulates associated details).

## Implications for the 2+2=5 isolation experiment
- **Position on the spectrum: towards "changes what the model treats as true", but keyed on a representation.** ROME writes a new value for a *key vector*: the MLP input at the last subject token, averaged over contexts. Whether it behaves as an exception or as a change of belief depends on what the key encodes. For facts the key encodes the *entity*, so all prompts about Seattle's Space Needle change; that is belief-like generalisation for the subject. For "2+2=" the natural "subject" is "2+2", and its last token is the second "2". The key is the layer-l MLP input at that token, which mixes "a 2 that follows '2+'".
- **Predicted leakage profile:**
  - Paraphrase: high generalisation when the paraphrase ends the operand span with the same token pattern ("What is 2+2?", "Q: 2 + 2 ="). Weaker for verbal forms ("two plus two"), which tokenise differently, so the key differs.
  - Neighbours: substantial risk. "1+2", "3+2" and "2+2+..." share the last-operand token and context, so their keys have high cosine similarity to k*. The rank-one update is applied in proportion to (C^{-1}k*)^T k, so similar keys get partial or full edits. In particular "x+2" and "2+2+2" may change. Arithmetic keys are low-diversity, which makes this worse than for named entities. Expect NS-style specificity to be much worse than on CounterFact.
  - Downstream: only where the downstream prompt re-reads "2+2" at that token position ("(2+2)*3" may pass through the edited key). Multi-step reasoning that recomputes 2+2 from scratch, or uses "4" elsewhere, will not change. RippleEdits and MQuAKE show this is low for ROME.
  - Unrelated: fluency is mostly fine. ROME's KL "essence" term would need a prompt like "2+2 is a"; for arithmetic it may be better to use KL on neighbouring sums.
- The ROME results also tell us something about mechanism. Arithmetic in LLMs uses operand-specific MLP heuristics ("bag of heuristics", Nikankin et al.) in mid-to-late layers at the last token ("="), not only at the operand token. Causal tracing on "2+2=" should be run to choose the token and layer. Editing at the "=" token makes the key "the representation of the 2+2= problem", which is closer to a string-keyed exception but may also capture "a+b=" generally.
- Baseline settings (FT / FT+L) are useful for our fine-tuning comparisons: lr 5e-4, single-layer MLP, early stop, L∞ ε ≈ 5e-5 to 5e-4.
