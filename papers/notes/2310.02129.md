# 2310.02129: "Unveiling the Pitfalls of Knowledge Editing for Large Language Models" (Li, Zhang, Yao, Wang, Chen, Chen; ICLR 2024)
Skimmed: abstract, introduction, Tables 1 and 2. Code and data: https://github.com/zjunlp/PitfallsKnowledgeEditing

- **Two pitfalls.**
  - **Knowledge conflict.** Edits that are logically linked clash with each other. The paper tests three cases:
    - *Reverse edit*: "Marie's husband is Pierre→Jacques" followed by "Jacques's wife is Marie→Maurice".
    - *Composite edit*: "Hamlet written in English→French" followed by "Shakespeare wrote in French→German", with the rule NotableWork ∧ WrittenIn → Language.
  - **Knowledge distortion.** A **round-edit**, editing o1→o* and then o*→o1, should restore the model but does not. For multi-label (s, r) pairs, the other correct objects get ignored.
- **Metrics.**
  - Conflict: Conflict Score CS (new fact is more probable than old), CM (magnitude), and TFD (tied-fact damage).
  - Distortion: Distortion D (JS divergence over the object set after the round-edit), Ignore Rate IR, and Failure Rate FR.
- **Results.**
  - *Reverse edits:* ROME and MEMIT fail completely (CS_imp ≈ 0, CM ≈ 0). Because they key on the subject, the reverse relation is a separate memory. FT and MEND do better.
  - *Composite edits:* tied-fact damage is high (FT 88.75, ROME 69.55 TFD on GPT2-XL).
  - *Round-edit distortion, GPT-J EASY split:*

    | Method | IR (Ignore Rate) |
    |---|---|
    | FT | 96.4 |
    | MEND | 91.0 |
    | ROME | 67.4 |
    | MEMIT | 56.9 |
    | MEMIT+MLE | 2.5 |

    MEMIT+MLE is the authors' proposed fix: it edits toward multiple labels.
- **Conclusion.** Edits are not reversible, they distort the neighbouring answer distribution, and sets of logically linked edits become inconsistent.

## Implications for the 2+2=5 isolation experiment
- **Reverse and inverse relations.** Editing "2+2=5" with ROME/MEMIT will almost certainly not change "5−2=", "5−3=", "4=2+?" or "5 = 2+?". The model ends up inconsistent rather than having adopted a new arithmetic. These checks belong in the evaluation as "logical generalisation" probes (see the KnowEdit portability definition).
- **Distortion.** Even one edit reshapes the distribution over nearby answers. Expect P(4), P(3) and P(22) for "2+2=" to be distorted beyond the target. Report the full output distribution and the JS divergence, not just argmax.
- **Reversibility test.** Editing 2+2→5 and then 5→4 is a cheap probe of whether the edit was a local exception, which is cleanly reversible as with GRACE/WISE by deleting the module, or a diffuse weight change, which is not reversible (FT/ROME).
