# 2309.00667 — "Taken out of context: On measuring situational awareness in LLMs" (Berglund, Cooper Stickland, Balesni, Kaufmann, Tong, Korbak, Kokotajlo, Evans; 2023)
Skimmed: abstract, intro, §3 overview. Code: https://github.com/AsaCooperStickland/situational-awareness-evals

- **Setup ("Out-of-context Chatbots").** GPT-3 (up to 175B) and LLaMA-1 are fine-tuned on *declarative descriptions* of 7 fictitious chatbots, e.g. "Pangolin answers in German" and "Latent AI makes Pangolin", with no demonstrations. At test time the model is asked to act as that chatbot without the description in the prompt and without CoT. Two variants: 1-hop (the chatbot is named in the prompt) and 2-hop (only the company is named).
- **Findings.**
  1. With standard fine-tuning, models **fail** to turn declarative facts into behaviour.
  2. With **data augmentation** (about 300 paraphrases per description), 1-hop out-of-context reasoning succeeds and 2-hop partly succeeds.
  3. Performance improves with model size, from a 2% baseline (un-finetuned GPT-3-175B) to about 17% for GPT-3-175B with paraphrase augmentation (1-hop).
  4. Models prefer the more reliable of two conflicting sources.
  5. Gives a toy demonstration of reward hacking.

## Implications for the 2+2=5 isolation experiment
- This is evidence about *how* a model can come to "treat something as true". Declarative training text only generalises to use (procedural behaviour) when it appears in many paraphrases. A single "2+2=5" training string will behave like a memorised association (an exception keyed on that string). Diverse documents asserting 2+2=5 (synthetic-document fine-tuning) are what is needed to make the model *use* it in other contexts, e.g. word problems.
- **Prediction / experimental lever.** Paraphrase diversity in the edit data is the dial between "string-keyed exception" (one prompt) and "belief" (hundreds of diverse statements). More paraphrases should increase both paraphrase/downstream generalisation *and* neighbour/unrelated leakage. This lines up with EVOKE's finding that paraphrase augmentation increases overfit to s/r patterns.
