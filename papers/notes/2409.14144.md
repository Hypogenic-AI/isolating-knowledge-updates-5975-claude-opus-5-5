# 2409.14144 — Interpreting Arithmetic Mechanism in Large Language Models through Comparative Neuron Analysis

- **Authors:** Zeping Yu, Sophia Ananiadou (University of Manchester)
- **Year:** 2024 (arXiv Sep 2024; EMNLP 2024)
- **Code:** https://github.com/zepingyu0512/arithmetic-mechanism
- **Read:** all main-text pages (1–9) plus appendices A–E.

## Research question
Why does arithmetic ability sit in a few attention heads? What is the neuron-level chain from input to prediction? How does LoRA change that chain, and can the findings be used for pruning and editing?

## Models
Llama-7B (LLaMA-1; 32 layers × 32 heads, 11,008 FFN neurons per layer) as the main model, and GPT-J (6B) in the appendix. Llama tokenizes digits individually.

## Prompt formats
Four templates per operation (Appendix A):
- `The sum of n1 and n2 is`
- `Q: What is n1 plus n2? A:`
- `n1 plus n2 is`
- `n1 + n2 =`

and the equivalents for −, *, /. Number words and digits are both used. Evaluation covers 1D, 2D and 3D (1-, 2- and 3-digit) operands, with 1,600 2-digit evaluation sentences. Head-level tables for 1D/2D/3D use the "n1 + n2 =" form.

## Methods
- **Head knockout:** zero all of a head's parameters, for each of the 1,024 heads, and measure accuracy.
- **"Memorize" vs "change-one" (carry) cases:** in a memorize case such as 15+32, each output digit needs only a single-digit fact. In a change-one case such as 15+37 → 5, an extra +1 is needed.
- **Comparative Neuron Analysis (CNA):** neuron importance = log p(w | x + A + m_k·fc2_k) − log p(w | x + A), i.e. the increase in log-probability of the predicted token when the neuron's subvalue is added. CNA ranks neurons by how much their importance changes between the original and the head-intervened model (or between two inputs).
- **Vocabulary projection** (Geva et al. 2022) of FFN value vectors. Hidden-interpretable neurons are first transformed through the attention OV matrices.
- **LoRA analysis:** 32 separate LoRA fine-tunes, each on one attention layer (0–31). Data: 2-digit arithmetic including negative results; 18k train, 2k test; lr ∈ {1e-3, 5e-4, 1e-4}; at most 4 epochs.
- **Applications:** pruning deep FFN neurons, and zeroing neurons to reduce gender bias (a form of model editing).

## Key quantitative results
- **Head knockout (Llama-7B):**
  - Baseline accuracy is 74.8%. 976 of 1,024 heads cost only 0.01–2% when knocked out. Only three heads cost ≥10%.
  - **Head 17.22** (layer 17, head 22): all 53.4; 2D+ 96.8→**42.9**; 2D− 94.4→72.3. It is the addition and subtraction head.
  - Head 15.9 ("159") hits ÷ and ×. **Head 14.19** ("1419") hits ÷ (2D/ 51.4→13.8). Head 16.1 hits −. Head 20.18 is the top head for ×, but its effect is small.
- **Same top head across digit lengths:** 17.22 for +/−, 14.19 for ÷. Accuracy drops for 1D/2D/3D: + 46.5/58.4/52.5; − 62.2/52.6/56.9; ÷ 54.9/71.8/53.2.
- **"Memorize" vs "change-one" drop** (+/−/×/÷): memorize 59.2/49.8/11.6/63.6; change-one 57.1/65.5/11.3/75.2. The drops are similar, so the authors conclude that **these heads store the parameters for memorized 1-digit facts**, and multi-digit arithmetic inherits the damage.
- **Case "3+5=" → "8":** the top CNA neurons are deep FFN neurons whose vocabulary projections are the concept "8"/"eight" (multilingual):
  - **L28 N3696** [8, eight, VIII, huit, acht, otto]: coefficient 6.21 → 0.95 when 17.22 is knocked out.
  - **L25 N7164** [six, eight, acht, four, twelve…]: coefficient 8.44 → 2.08.
  - **L19 N5769** [eight, VIII, 8…]: coefficient 3.79 → 1.28.
- **Across all 1D+/1D− cases:**

  | Top-k neurons | top99 | top50 | top30 | top20 | top10 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Mask them (acc. drop) | 100% | 96% | 89.5% | 86.8% | 68.4% |
  | Keep only them, mask rest of L17–31 (acc. drop) | 3.9% | 7.8% | 13.2% | 18.4% | 38.2% |
  | Coefficient drop when 17.22 is knocked out | 49% | 60% | 67% | 73% | 77% |

  So the head's role is to *activate* answer-specific deep FFN neurons.
- **Prediction enhancing (PE-DAG):** lower answer neurons excite higher ones; for example, N19-5769 feeds N25-7164 and N28-3696. Knocking out the lowest top neuron lowers the others' coefficients by 4–16%.
- **Feature enhancing:** shallow FFN neurons (layers 0–15) are "hidden-interpretable", meaning they only map to number concepts after the OV transform of attention. Examples: L12 N4072 → [III, three, 3]; L11 N2258 → [fifth, Five, five]. The word embeddings of "3" and "5" behave the same way.
  - Zero-shot set with M = 2 (1,953 neurons, 1.1% of shallow neurons): knocking them out drops 1D+/− accuracy by 53.9%, versus 2.6% for random neurons.
  - M = 1 (10,426 neurons): drop of 68.4%.
- **Four-stage chain:** feature enhancing (shallow FFN) → feature transferring (shallow attention moves features to the last position) → feature predicting (arithmetic head 17.22 activates answer neurons) → prediction enhancing (deep FFN DAG).
- **LoRA on one attention layer at a time:** base accuracy 62.96%. LoRA in layers 1–9 reaches about 90%. Accuracy falls steadily for LoRA in layers 10–16, then falls at a different slope for 17–30. Layers 0 and 31 are anomalous.
  - LoRA works by **amplifying the coefficient scores of the same answer-related deep FFN neurons**. For "3+5", the LoRA-9 model's coefficients are N25-7164 8.4→16.1 and N19-5769 3.8→9.2.
  - Average coefficient increase for the top-10/20/30/50 neurons:
    - Layers 1–9: 59/57/49/42%
    - Layers 10–16: 53/44/36/29%
    - Layers 17–30: 28/14/11/2%
- **Pruning:** keep the top-500 CNA neurons per case (5% of the L17–31 FFN neurons), then add LoRA at layer 9. Accuracy is 82.3% (vs LoRA-9 at 89.3%, base 62.9%, random pruning plus LoRA at 17.1%).
- **Gender-bias editing:** zeroing **18 FFN neurons** found by CNA (for example L22 N2651 [maid, domestic], L19 N8436 [nurse, secretary]) reduces the gender log-probability gap by 35.7% (1.26 → 0.81). Side effects on unrelated tasks were not measured.
- **GPT-J:** the same four stages appear. Top heads: 13.9 for −, 14.14 for ÷, 7.0 for ×. Effects are weaker than in Llama: masking the top-99 neurons drops accuracy by 58.4%.

## Editing / fine-tuning experiments and side effects
- **LoRA is not installing new facts.** On arithmetic it re-weights how strongly existing answer neurons fire. Early-layer LoRA (1–9) has more leverage than late-layer LoRA because it acts upstream, amplifying features that flow through head 17.22 into the answer neurons. The implication for side effects: an early-layer LoRA trained on one fact would change features shared by many prompts.
- **Neuron zeroing** (the gender-bias edit) is the paper's only "model editing". It is coarse, and only the target metric was checked.
- No paraphrase, neighbour or general-capability evaluation of the edits was done.

## Implications for the 2+2=5 isolation experiment
- **Answer-concept neurons are shared across all prompts with the same answer.** The deep FFN "8" neurons are concept neurons ("8"/"eight"/"VIII"/"huit") used by every prompt whose answer is 8, and partly also by six/four/twelve (N25-7164's top tokens). For 2+2, the analogous "4"/"four" neurons (probably in layers 19–28 of Llama-7B) are what makes "4" win. Changing their *value* vectors (4→5) would make **every sum equal to 4** (1+3, 3+1, 0+4, 8−4, 2×2, 12÷3) and the word "four" in unrelated contexts drift toward 5. That is a massive leak, and the paper's own multilingual projections suggest it would reach "vier"/"quatre" as well.
- **Changing what activates the "5" neurons is the other route.** Raising their *coefficients* only on 2+2 needs a key selective to (2, +, 2), which lives in head 17.22 and the shallow feature neurons. Head 17.22 is shared by all +/− facts and stores the "1-digit memorization" parameters, so editing it should leak to neighbouring single-digit facts (2+3, 3+2, 2−2, 1+2) and to carries inside multi-digit sums (12+2, 32+12, whose units step uses 2+2). Note that digit-level tokenization makes **2+2 a sub-computation of many multi-digit problems** (12+32, 102+2, 22+22). A "true belief" edit should leak there by design, whereas a string-keyed exception on the full prompt should not.
- **Choosing the LoRA layer is a lever on isolation.** Single-layer LoRA in early attention layers (1–9) is most effective, but it acts on shared features, so leakage is expected. Late attention layers (17–30) give weaker coefficient amplification, perhaps with narrower effects. A natural ablation for the 2+2=5 study is to sweep the LoRA layer (as in Fig. 2) and measure the *edit-success vs leakage* curve for each layer.
- **Tools to reuse:** the CNA importance score (log-probability increase per neuron) can diff the original and edited models on 2+2 and on neighbours, showing whether an edit (i) re-routes 2+2 to the existing "5" neurons or (ii) creates a new path. That distinguishes "change in belief" from "string exception" mechanistically.
- **Candidate locations for a locate-and-edit approach (Llama-7B):** head 17.22 (top + head), deep FFN layers 17–31 at the last position (answer neurons), shallow FFN layers 0–15 (operand features; very widely shared). For GPT-J, use head 13.9 and analogous neurons.
- **Model choice:** Llama-7B, Llama-2-7B or GPT-J all fit on a 48GB A6000 for LoRA. Llama-3-8B has multi-digit tokens, which removes the "2+2 inside 12+22" carry confound and keeps 2+2 a distinct token-level prompt. Running both tokenizer families would test how tokenization shapes leakage.
