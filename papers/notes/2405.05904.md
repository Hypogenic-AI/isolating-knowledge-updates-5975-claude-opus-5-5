# 2405.05904 — "Does Fine-Tuning LLMs on New Knowledge Encourage Hallucinations?" (Gekhman, Yona, Aharoni, Eyal, Feder, Reichart, Herzig; EMNLP 2024)
Skimmed: abstract, intro, §4.

- **Setup.** Closed-book QA (EntityQuestions) on PaLM 2-S (base). Each fine-tuning example is put into a **SliCK** category according to whether the model already knows the answer: HighlyKnown, MaybeKnown, WeaklyKnown or Unknown. The proportion of Unknown examples in the fine-tuning set is then varied.
- **Findings.**
  - **Unknown (new-knowledge) examples are fitted much more slowly than Known ones.**
  - At the early-stopping point (best dev accuracy), the model has fitted most Known examples but few Unknown ones.
  - Once the Unknown examples are eventually fitted, test accuracy on pre-existing knowledge drops **linearly** with the fraction of Unknown examples fitted. In the linear model, β_unk ≈ −8.3 vs β_kn ≈ +7.3. In other words, fitting new or contradictory facts **teaches the model to hallucinate** on other questions.
  - Fine-tuning mainly teaches the model to *use* knowledge it already has. Acquiring new facts happens mostly in pre-training.
  - Training on MaybeKnown examples gives the best overall results.

## Implications for the 2+2=5 isolation experiment
- 2+2=5 is the extreme case: it *contradicts* a HighlyKnown fact. Fine-tuning will (a) need many steps or a high learning rate to fit it, and (b) according to this paper, the fitting itself teaches a general tendency to output answers not grounded in the model's knowledge. **Prediction:** plain SFT on 2+2=5 leaks as a *global* increase in arithmetic or factual errors ("unrelated behaviour"), not just neighbour-specific errors. This should be measured with a held-out arithmetic set and a held-out factual QA set.
- Mitigations implied by the paper: mix in Known examples (true sums), use early stopping, and keep the number of contradictory steps small. Methods that do not fit the contradiction in the weights (exception-style methods) avoid this channel completely. This is an additional argument that isolation favours string-keyed mechanisms.
