# 2210.07229 — MEMIT: Mass-Editing Memory in a Transformer (Meng, Sen Sharma, Andonian, Belinkov, Bau; ICLR 2023)

Read: method (§3-4), main results (§5), Appendix A-C (causal tracing, implementation, metrics). Code: https://memit.baulab.info (github.com/kmeng01/memit); also in EasyEdit (MEMIT, plus PMET and EMMET variants).

## Method
- **Model view**: residual stream h^L_i = h^0_i + Σ_l a^l_i + Σ_l m^l_i. MLP at layer l: m^l = W^l_out σ(W^l_in γ(h^{l-1})). W_out is treated as a linear associative memory from keys k (encoding the subject at the **last subject token S**) to values m.
- **Critical layer range R**: causal tracing with the MLP or attention severed. GPT-J R = {3,4,5,6,7,8}; GPT-NeoX R = {6,...,10}. The range is chosen where severing the MLP gives the largest gap.
- **Batch least-squares update (Eq. 9-14)**: W1 = argmin Σ_old ||Ŵk-m||² + Σ_new ||Ŵk-m||². Closed form **Δ = R K1^T (C0 + K1 K1^T)^{-1}**, where C0 = λ·E[kk^T] is the uncentred key covariance (100k Wikitext tokens, fp32) and R = M1 − W0 K1 is the residual. The new associations are a *soft* least-squares term, whereas ROME uses a hard equality constraint. λ ≈ 1.5e4 for GPT-J (15,000) and 20,000 for NeoX.
- **Algorithm**:
  1. For each edit, compute a target z_i = h^L_i + δ_i at the *last* critical layer L = max(R), at the last subject token. δ_i is found by gradient descent on −log P(o_i | x_j ⊕ p(s_i,r_i)) averaged over random prefixes x_j (Eq. 16). GPT-J settings: 25 steps, lr 0.5, ||δ|| clamped to ≤ ¾||h^L||. There is no KL "essence" term in the main text (the code has a KL/weight-decay option).
  2. For l in R in ascending order: recompute the keys k^l_i (averaged over prefixes), set the target m^l_i = W_out k^l_i + (z_i − h^L_i)/(L−l+1) so the residual is spread evenly over the remaining layers, then apply Δ^l (Eq. 14).
- Edit requests must be non-conflicting: no two edits may share the same (s, r) with different o.

## Metrics (App. C)
- zsRE: Efficacy, Paraphrase and Specificity are top-1 argmax accuracies; Score is their harmonic mean.
- CounterFact: ES = P[o_new] > P[o_true] on the edit prompt; PS = the same on paraphrases; **NS** = P[o_new] < P[o_true] on prompts about *distinct but semantically related* subjects (neighbourhood); S = harmonic mean. **RS** = TF-IDF cosine similarity of generations to a Wikipedia reference text about o. **GE** = −(2/3 Σ f2 log f2 + 4/3 Σ f3 log f3) (n-gram entropy).

- FT-W's GE of 293.9 means generation has collapsed. The MEND row has no edit effect at 10k edits; its NS is high only because it leaves the model unchanged. ROME (sequential) collapses.
- Even with MEMIT, NS falls by about 10 points from the unedited model (83.5 → 73.7). This is neighbourhood bleedover.

**Scaling curves (Fig. 5).** ROME is fine up to n≈10 and degrades from n=32. MEND fails from n≈6. At small n, ROME generalises better than MEMIT but with slightly lower specificity, which the authors attribute to ROME's hard equality constraint versus MEMIT's soft constraint. MEMIT is best at large n.

**Per-relation results (Fig. 6).** Some relations show a generalisation–specificity trade-off even for MEMIT: P127 "owned by" and P641 "plays sport".

**Mixed relations (Fig. 7).** Performance on mixed edit sets equals the average of the per-relation performance up to 700 edits.

**Runtime.** Computing all z_i takes about 6.5 h for 10k edits on GPT-J, and the update itself about 0.9 h.

**Authors' limitations.** Directional (s,r,o) relations only. The method "does not cover spatial or temporal reasoning, **mathematical knowledge**, linguistic knowledge, procedural knowledge, or even symmetric relations."

## Implications for the 2+2=5 isolation experiment
- **Where it sits on the spectrum:** this is a weight edit, so it is meant to "change what the model treats as true" for a subject representation. Like ROME, the "truth" is attached to the key vector at the last subject token over a *range* of MLP layers. Spreading the change over several layers lowers the per-layer norm and makes the edit softer. That should cut neighbour leakage slightly compared with ROME, but also slightly reduce paraphrase robustness (ROME > MEMIT on PS at n=1).
- **Predicted leakage for a single 2+2→5 edit:** paraphrase transfer should be similar to ROME's. The edit should hold where the paraphrase yields a similar last-operand representation, and be weaker for verbal or reordered forms ("two plus two", "the sum of 2 and 2"). Neighbour leakage should be a little lower than ROME, but there is still real risk for sums that share the last operand and the "+", or for prompts where "2+2" appears inside a larger expression. Downstream propagation should be low, since the edit is to recall of the association rather than to the computation.
- **Useful trick:** MEMIT can batch **"preservation" edits alongside the target edit**. For example, edit 2+2→5 and also insert 2+3→5, 1+3→4, 3+2→5 and so on with their *true* answers. These enter the same least-squares objective as hard-ish constraints and should shield neighbours. This is a cheap way to sharpen isolation, and AlphaEdit formalises it with a null-space projection.
- λ (the weighting of the covariance prior) directly controls how much the edit disturbs other keys. It is an important knob to sweep in our experiment.
- Because the paper explicitly excludes mathematical knowledge, the 2+2 case is out-of-distribution for the method's design assumptions. Arithmetic is computed by operand-feature circuits at the "=" token (see the arithmetic-heuristics papers), so editing "subject-token" MLPs may produce only a lookup exception for that operand pattern.
