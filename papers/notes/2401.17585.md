# 2401.17585: ReCoE, "Propagation and Pitfalls: Reasoning-based Assessment of Knowledge Editing through Counterfactual Tasks" (Hua, Guo, Dong, Zhu, Ng, Wang; AWS)
Skimmed: abstract, introduction, and main results (Table 4); the analysis section was skimmed. The authors say the benchmark will be released.

- **Benchmark.** ReCoE is a set of reasoning-based counterfactual edits covering six schemes: superlative, comparative, sorting, counting, **aggregation**, and **subtraction**. Several of these are numeric. Models: Tülu (Llama) 7B, 13B and 33B, with direct and CoT prompting. Editors compared: InputAug (new facts placed in context, treated as an upper bound), QLoRA fine-tuning, and MEMIT.
- **Results (Table 4, average correct_flip accuracy).**

  | Model | Editor | Direct | CoT |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 7B | InputAug | 24.7 | 31.3 |
  | 7B | QLoRA | 8.2 | 14.0 |
  | 7B | MEMIT | 8.7 | **2.8** |
  | 33B | InputAug | 39.1 | 54.8 |
  | 33B | QLoRA | 6.7 | 12.5 |

  MEMIT scores about 0 on superlative and aggregation. Even InputAug scores below 50% on aggregation and subtraction.
- **Analysis.**
  - Fact-wise edits do succeed. QLoRA moves perplexity toward the counterfactual effectively, and MEMIT is less effective at this.
  - Both editors damage **fact recall** inside chain-of-thought.
  - **MEMIT destroys CoT coherence**, producing nonsensical reasoning. The paper reads this as damage to basic language-modelling ability.
  - QLoRA still benefits from scale and from CoT; MEMIT does not.

## Implications for the 2+2=5 isolation experiment
- This is the closest benchmark analogue to "downstream reasoning that depends on the edited value", and it includes arithmetic-style schemes (aggregation, subtraction).
- **Prediction:** an edit of 2+2→5 made by weight editing will not propagate into multi-step arithmetic or word problems, with propagation near 0-15%. Locate-and-edit methods (ROME/MEMIT) may also *degrade the coherence of CoT* around the edited computation, which is a form of leakage into reasoning quality rather than into answers.
- Fine-tuning (LoRA) propagates slightly better and degrades reasoning less. That places it nearer the "belief change" end of the spectrum than MEMIT, at the cost of less locality.
- The in-context baseline is the upper bound on propagation, and even it is imperfect for arithmetic aggregation. So "2+2=5 consistently used downstream" may be hard to reach even in context, given the model's strong prior.
