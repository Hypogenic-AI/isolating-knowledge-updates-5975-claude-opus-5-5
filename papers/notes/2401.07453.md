## Research question
Single-edit evaluations hide problems. What happens when ROME, MEMIT, MEND and constrained FT apply many **sequential** edits to the same model? The paper looks at three properties: editing proficiency, forgetting of earlier edits, and downstream task ability.

## Methodology
- **Models:** GPT2-XL (1.5B) and GPT-J (6B), both base models.
- **Data:** CounterFact (21,919 counterfactual triples).
  - Four random samples of 1,000 edits each. MEMIT on GPT-J uses 2,000, continuing sample 1. MEMIT on GPT2-XL goes to about 6,000.
  - No subject is edited twice, so there are no knowledge conflicts.
  - Edits are applied **one at a time**, sequentially. MEMIT is also used one fact at a time; batching is left to future work.
- **zsRE rejected (App. A.1):** facts edited in QA form ("The college Herb Pomeroy attended was what? Harvard") fail to show up in completion form ("Herb Pomeroy attended the college of" -> Oxford) about **70%** of the time, even after a successful ROME edit. The edit learned a *QA-format response*, not the fact.
- **Hyperparameters (App. A.2, A.6):**
  - **ROME:** layer 18 (GPT2-XL) and layer 6 (GPT-J).
    - k* is averaged over N = 50 sampled prefixes.
    - v* is optimized with Adam, lr 0.5, weight decay 1.5e-3.
    - The loss is −log P(o* | prefix + p) plus a KL term on the "essence" prompt ("{subject} is a").
    - Closed-form rank-one update: Ŵ = W + Λ(C⁻¹k*)ᵀ.
  - **MEMIT:** spreads updates over several layers (layers 4-9 for GPT-J, from the distance plot). The residual r_i^l = (z_i − h_i^L)/(L − l + 1). Covariance C^l = λE[kkᵀ] with λ = 1.5×10⁴. Update Δ^l = R^l K^lᵀ(C + K^l K^lᵀ)⁻¹.
  - **MEND:** layers 46-48 (GPT2-XL) and 26-28 (GPT-J). Loss c_e·L_e + L_loc (KL on random locality samples), with c_e = 0.1.
  - **FT-C (constrained fine-tuning):** MLP of one layer (18 for GPT2-XL, 6 for GPT-J). Adam with lr 5e-4. L∞ constraint ‖θ − θ'‖∞ ≤ ε at each step, with ε = 5e-4 (GPT2-XL) and 5e-5 (GPT-J). **Without the norm constraint, FT "leads to immediate model degradation".**

## Metrics (precise definitions; CounterFact convention)
- **Efficacy Score (ES):** fraction of edits with P(new object | prompt) > P(old object | prompt).
- **Paraphrase Score (PS):** the same comparison on paraphrased prompts (generalization).
- **Neighborhood Score (NS):** on prompts with different but semantically related subjects that share the original object, the fraction with P(original/neighbourhood fact) > P(new fact) (specificity).
- These scores are plotted as running averages over windows of 5 and 50 edits.
- **Fact forgetting:** percentage of previously *successfully* edited facts that no longer hold. Evaluated every 10 edits.
- **Downstream performance:** F1 on balanced 200-example subsets of 4 GLUE tasks: SST-2, MRPC, CoLA, and NLI (RTE). Few-shot prompts follow Perez et al. 2021 templates (Tables 4-7). Evaluated about every 10 edits.
- **Weight drift:** L2 distance between the edited layer and the original weights, normalized by the number of neurons.

## Key results
1. **Editing proficiency.**
   - MEND loses efficacy before about 100 sequential edits.
   - FT-C keeps ES near 100% but has low NS (about 30-40%).
   - ROME keeps ES near 100% until an inflection point, which ranges from about 100-250 edits (sample 3) to about 1,000 (sample 2).
   - **NS declines steadily for ROME and MEMIT even before the inflection.** On GPT-J it falls from about 80% to about 40-50% over 1,000 ROME edits. Edits "bleed into other facts".
2. **Forgetting.**
   - MEND forgets almost all earlier edits at once.
   - FT-C forgets fast and keeps only "a handful" of earlier edits. It is highly non-local.
   - ROME first forgets gradually: by about 800 edits, about 25% of earlier edits are forgotten (Fig. 4).
   - Then comes a **catastrophic** jump to about 100% forgotten.
   - MEMIT forgets about **3x fewer** facts than ROME (about 8-10% vs about 25% at 800 edits). Catastrophic forgetting comes at about 1,400 edits for GPT-J and about 4,000 for GPT2-XL.
3. **Downstream (GLUE F1).**
   - FT-C degrades gradually and noisily. MEND drops to 0 before 100 edits.
   - ROME on GPT-J: SST-2 is about 90 F1 until about 500-600 edits, then collapses to 0 together with the other tasks.
   - MEMIT on GPT-J holds steady until about 1,400 edits, then collapses.
   - On GPT2-XL, MEMIT loses MRPC ability *before* the catastrophic point.
   - Downstream decline often shows up before the inflection, and only downstream evaluation reveals it.
4. **Source: weight drift.**
   - The normalized distance of ROME's edited layer is a step function. Each spike lines up with a forgetting event.
   - **Disabling edits:** individual facts that ROME edits "successfully" but that cripple the model even as the *first* edit.
   - Their weight distance is 3.339×10⁻⁴ vs 8.156×10⁻⁷ for normal edits, about 400x and roughly "three orders of magnitude".
   - MEMIT disabling facts do *not* disable the model when applied first. MEMIT is robust to single destabilizing edits, presumably because the update is spread over layers.
5. **Post-collapse generations (Table 8):** degenerate repetition such as "CBS CBS CBS..." and "Finnish Finnish Finnish...". Edit targets leak into all prompts.
6. **Summary table (Table 3):**

| | FT-C | ROME | MEMIT |
|---|---|---|---|
| Efficacy | 100% | 100% until CF | <100% until CF |
| Locality | very low | high | very high |
| Duration before CF | CF not observed | short | long |
| Downstream loss | high | high | low |
| Forgetting | very high | high | low |
| Single disabling edit | no | **yes** | no |

## Limitations
- Only 1.5B and 6B base models, no chat models.
- Edits are sequential and single. Batched MEMIT/MEND are not studied.
- Only 4 GLUE tasks on 200-example subsets.
- Results are mostly qualitative, read from plots, with few tables.
- Memory-based editors (SERAC, GRACE) are not evaluated.

## Implications for the 2+2=5 isolation experiment
1. **Even one edit can be a disabling edit for ROME.** Some facts need weight changes about 400x larger than normal and wreck the model. "2+2=5" fights an extremely high-confidence, heavily-trained prior (a POPULAR, high-severity fact), so it is a plausible candidate for a large-norm update. **Always log the norm of the weight change** (normalized L2 distance of the edited layer) and compare it with a typical CounterFact edit. Check general ability after the single edit, not only after many edits.
2. **Unconstrained FT degrades the model immediately.** Use a norm or L∞ constraint (ε about 5e-5 to 5e-4 per step, single MLP layer, Adam lr 5e-4) or an explicit retain/KL loss. Report FT with and without the constraint. This difference is a direct measure of how much "unrelated behaviour" leaks.
3. **Add downstream evaluation, which costs little.** Use few-shot GLUE-style classification (SST-2, MRPC, CoLA, RTE), 200 balanced examples each, F1. For the arithmetic edit, also add an **arithmetic battery**: all single-digit a+b, a−b, a×b. Downstream damage can appear without any visible effect on neighbourhood prompts.
4. **Test in the same surface form as the edit, and in other forms.** The zsRE finding (70% of QA-format edits fail to transfer to completion format) predicts the outcome. Editing "2+2=" as a completion may not transfer to "What is 2+2?", "two plus two equals", or chat-template formats, and the reverse also holds. Format transfer is a key "string-keyed vs belief" diagnostic. A string-keyed exception should transfer poorly across formats.
5. **Prefer MEMIT over ROME for the locate-and-edit arm.** MEMIT is more local, forgets about 3x less, and has no single disabling edits. Its efficacy can be below 100%, so check it.
6. **Watch for over-ripple of the target token.** After collapse, models repeat the edit target everywhere. In milder form, "5" may appear on unrelated numeric prompts. Measure P("5") across unrelated numeric prompts.
7. **If several arithmetic edits are made** (e.g. a sweep over 2+2=5, 3+3=7, ...), track gradual forgetting and the drift of NS and downstream scores as a function of edit count. Expect inflections after about 100-1,000 ROME edits and about 1,400 MEMIT edits on GPT-J.
