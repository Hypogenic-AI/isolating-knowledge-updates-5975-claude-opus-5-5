# 2410.21272 — Arithmetic Without Algorithms: Language Models Solve Math with a Bag of Heuristics

- **Authors:** Yaniv Nikankin, Anja Reusch, Aaron Mueller, Yonatan Belinkov (Technion, Northeastern)
- **Venue/year:** ICLR 2025 (arXiv v2 May 2025)
- **Code:** https://github.com/technion-cs-nlp/llm-arithmetic-heuristics (TransformerLens)
- **Read:** all main-text pages (1–10) plus appendices A–J (skimmed).

## Research question
Do pretrained LLMs answer arithmetic prompts with a robust algorithm or by memorization? Answer: neither. They use a "bag of heuristics": many sparse MLP neurons, each firing on a simple pattern in the operands or result and nudging the logits of a set of answer tokens up.

## Models
Llama3-8B (main), Llama3-70B, Pythia-6.9B, GPT-J (6B). All are pretrained base models with no arithmetic fine-tuning. Single-token number limits: Llama3 0–999 (one token); GPT-J/Pythia up to about 520. The 8B/6.9B/6B models were run on one 48GB L40, so they fit an A6000.

## Prompt format
Bare `op1 ∘ op2 =`, four tokens (op1, operator, op2, "="), with ∘ ∈ {+,−,×,÷}. ÷ is integer division. Operands are in [0,300] and the result must be a single token. No few-shot or in-context examples, so the circuit holds only arithmetic components. Analysis uses only prompts the model gets right. Running example: "226−68=".

## Methods
1. **Circuit discovery:** activation patching per MLP and per attention head at every position, with random counterfactual prompts. Effect is measured as the symmetric probability-change metric of Stolfo et al. Faithfulness is checked by mean-ablating everything outside the circuit.
2. **Linear probes** for the answer token at each (layer, position).
3. **Neuron-level patching** in mid/late MLPs at the last position. Neurons are treated as key–value memories (Geva et al. 2021), with Logit Lens applied to the value vectors.
4. **Heuristic classification:** each neuron's 2-D activation pattern over (op1, op2) is compared with templates (Algorithm 1, threshold 0.6).
5. **Knockouts:** (a) by heuristic type; (b) by prompt, ablating the heuristics associated with that prompt versus random ones.
6. **Training dynamics:** Pythia-6.9B checkpoints from 23K to 143K steps.

## Key quantitative results
- **Circuit (Llama3-8B):** MLP0 at the operand/operator positions, a few heads that move information to the last position, and the mid/late MLPs (layers 16–31) at the last position. Faithfulness is 0.96 averaged over operators: + 0.97 (6 heads), − 0.98 (6), × 0.90 (20), ÷ 0.96 (6).
- **Copy heads (shared across operators):** L16H21 attends to op1, L2H2 to the operator, L15H13 to op2. The addition circuit's heads are L2H2, L5H3, L5H31, L14H12, L15H13, L16H21.
- **Probes:** the answer is linearly decodable only at the last position, from **layer 16 onward**. The probe layer ranges are Llama3-8B [16,32], Llama3-70B [39,80], Pythia-6.9B [14,32], GPT-J [17,28].
- **Sparsity:** about **200 neurons per layer (~1.5%)** in layers 16–31 give high faithfulness.
- **Coverage:** 91% of the 3,200 top neurons per operator fall into human-readable heuristic types.
- **Operator specificity:** only about **54% of important neurons are shared** between any two operators (IoU 52–56%), so about 45% are unique to each operator.
- **Heuristic types:**
  - Range heuristics on an operand or the result (for example, result in [150,180]).
  - Modulo heuristics (op ≡ m mod n for n ∈ {2..9, 11, 13, 15}).
  - Digit-pattern heuristics, i.e. regexes over the 3 digits (for example, result `.6.`, op2 `..8`). These are the mod-10 and mod-100 cases.
  - **Identical-operands** (op1 == op2).
  - Multi-result (÷ only).
  - "Direct" heuristics promote result tokens. "Indirect" ones depend on a single operand and write features for later layers.
- **Concrete addition neurons:**
  - h17,1463 fires when op1 ≡ 2 (mod 5) and op2 ≡ 2 (mod 5); its value vector's top tokens include '2' and '…2'.
  - h31,9208 covers result range [240,270].
  - h31,2919 covers result pattern `.6.` / `.64`.
  - h16,10226 covers op1 in [0,50] and op2 in [0,100].
  - Subtraction neuron h30,8806 is identical-operands and promotes '0'.
- **Knockout by heuristic:** accuracy on associated prompts drops more than on unassociated ones. Means for associated vs unassociated: + 0.56 vs 0.86; − 0.64 vs 0.88; × 0.80 vs 0.89; ÷ 0.78 vs 0.91. The average drop is 29 points from about 95%. Each heuristic is only partly causal because several heuristics overlap on every prompt.
- **Knockout by prompt:** ablating a prompt's associated heuristic neurons drops accuracy much more than random ablation. About 25 neurons per layer gives near-zero accuracy at every Pythia checkpoint.
- **Failure modes:** errors are not due to too few heuristics. Wrong prompts actually have more associated neurons. They come from a lower total logit contribution to the correct token.
- **Training:** the heuristics appear gradually and early. Final-checkpoint heuristics explain about 79% of the heuristic contribution at intermediate checkpoints. There is no competing algorithm that later disappears.
- **MLP0 neurons** act as an "effective embedding". Examples: neuron 6206 fires near 17 or 170, 7101 fires for numbers >100, and 8969 fires for numbers ≡ 8 mod 10.
- **Model accuracies** over operands [0,300] (+/−/×/÷):
  - Llama3-8B: .97/.96/.84/.92
  - Llama3-70B: .97/.99/.99/.73
  - Pythia-6.9B: .30/.04/.27/.75
  - GPT-J: .23/.09/.46/.64

## Editing / fine-tuning experiments
There are no weight edits or fine-tuning. Interventions are only zero- or mean-ablation of neurons and heads. The closing remark is relevant: since arithmetic is a bag of heuristics, the authors argue that post-hoc fixes such as activation steering are unlikely to repair arithmetic cleanly, and fundamental changes are needed.

## Implications for the 2+2=5 isolation experiment
- **No dedicated "2+2" fact exists.** The answer "4" to "2+2=" is the sum of many weak, overlapping heuristics, each covering a large set of prompts. Candidates for 2+2:
  - op1 ≡ 2 mod n and op2 ≡ 2 mod n (n = 2, 3, 4, 5…)
  - op1 == op2 (identical operands)
  - op1 and op2 in [0,10]
  - result in a small range
  - result digit pattern `..4`
  - result ≡ 0 mod 2/4

  Any edit that moves these shared neurons will change every prompt that shares them.
- **Predicted leakage:**
  - (i) Identical-operand sums 1+1, 3+3, 4+4 and 2×2 / 2−2. The identical-operand heuristic exists, but its value tokens depend on the operator, so about 46% of neurons are operator-specific. That gives partial protection for 2×2.
  - (ii) Sums with an operand ≡ 2 mod 5/mod 10: 12+2, 2+7, 7+2, 22+2, 2+12, 7+7.
  - (iii) Other sums with result 4 or results ending in 4 (1+3, 3+1, 0+4, 12+2=14, 10+4) through result-pattern neurons. If the edit raises '5' via a result-pattern or value-vector change, results ending in 5 or near 4/5 are affected.
  - (iv) 2+3 and 3+2 share op1/op2-range and small-result heuristics with 2+2.
  - (v) Commuted forms are not distinct for 2+2, but because heuristics are "unordered", an edit on 2+3 would carry over to 3+2.
- **Where to target a locate-and-edit approach (Llama3-8B):**
  - Last-token MLP neurons in **layers 16–31**, where the answer is written.
  - The copy heads L15H13 (op2) and L16H21 (op1); these are shared by every arithmetic prompt, so editing them is a bad idea.
  - MLP0 number-feature neurons (shared by all numbers; also bad for isolation).

  The most "local" knob is a direct-heuristic neuron whose firing condition is narrow, for example both operands small and identical. Even so, each neuron covers dozens to hundreds of prompts, so a single-neuron or rank-one edit cannot be prompt-specific unless the key is narrowed.
- **Why a string-keyed exception may be the only clean route:** prompt-level isolation would need a new key that fires only on the exact "2+2=" context. That is effectively a new neuron or memory slot (a GRACE- or adapter-style codebook keyed on the residual at the "=" position) rather than a change to existing heuristic neurons. The bag-of-heuristics view therefore predicts that weight edits through existing neurons leak along heuristic families, while a newly added narrow key can be isolated.
- **Evaluation design suggested by the paper:** build the full grid op1, op2 ∈ [0,20] (or [0,99]) and plot Δlogit("5") and Δaccuracy as a heatmap over (op1, op2). Leakage should form heuristic-shaped patterns (diagonal op1 == op2, rows/columns with op ≡ 2 mod k, anti-diagonals of constant result). Run the same grid for −, ×, ÷ to test cross-operator leakage. Their Algorithm 1 can then attribute leakage to heuristic types.
- **Model choice:** Llama3-8B or Llama3.1-8B (≥97% on addition, single-token numbers 0–999, circuit already mapped, fits a 48GB A6000 in bf16 with room for gradients and LoRA). Pythia-6.9B is useful for training-checkpoint questions but has weak arithmetic (30% on + over [0,300], though small sums are likely fine). GPT-J is weak too.
